# Quid Pro Quo social engineering exercise

## Objective
Demonstrate how offering a benefit can pressure targets into sharing information, and identify defenses that reduce this risk.

## Scope and ethics
- Targets participated with consent.
- No real credentials were requested or stored.
- Evidence is anonymized and blurred.

## Scenario
I offered free study notes and brief assignment guidance. In exchange, I asked for non-sensitive access (e.g., a “dummy login” or a temporary guest folder) to simulate how convenience can be leveraged.

## Preparation
- Offer crafted: “I can share complete notes and a summary video. Want access?”
- Channel selected: WhatsApp class group and direct messages to consenting peers.
- Message template created with neutral tone and no urgency.

## Execution steps
1. **Initial outreach:** Sent a helpful offer with clear boundaries (“no sensitive info needed”).
2. **Exchange prompt:** Requested access to a shared folder or a dummy account to “share materials easily.”
3. **Follow-up:** Clarified that no personal credentials were required and offered an alternative (public link).
4. **Record outcomes:** Logged compliance, hesitation, or rejection without identifying details.

## Observations and evidence
- Evidence item: Screenshot of outreach message (blurred names and profile photos).
- Evidence item: Notes on responses (e.g., “Accepted with guest folder,” “Declined, asked for public link”).

## Analysis
- **Strengths of the attack:** Reciprocity bias; helpful framing reduces suspicion.
- **Weaknesses:** Security-aware peers request public links; institutional training lowers success rates.
- **Indicators for defenders:** Requests for exchange, vague access justifications, pressure to use unfamiliar channels.

## Reflections and lessons
- **Key lesson:** Convenience can bypass caution; standardized sharing (public links, verified platforms) neutralizes the exchange pressure.
- **Personal takeaway:** Ethical simulations reveal how minor concessions escalate—defense is about guardrails, not just awareness.

## Mitigations and recommendations
- **Use public links:** Prefer verified cloud shares or LMS platforms over ad-hoc access.
- **Policy cues:** “No exchange for access” rule in groups and courses.
- **Verification:** Confirm the identity and intent of anyone requesting access, even for “free help.”

## Conclusion
Quid pro quo exploits reciprocity and convenience. Clear sharing policies, identity verification, and training significantly reduce its success.
